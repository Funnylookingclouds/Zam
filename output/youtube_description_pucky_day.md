## Description
Ever wondered what an NHL puck does all day? Freezer at -10 °C, warm-ups, a 108.8 mph slap shot and a shift change every couple of minutes – a day at work with Pucky! 🧊🏒

🧊 **Frozen on purpose:** NHL game pucks are kept frozen (around -10 to -7 °C / 14–20 °F) because cold rubber bounces less and glides better.
The Hockey News: https://thehockeynews.com/news/news/frozen-pucks-key-to-better-games

⏱️ **Shift change:** a puck in play for a couple of minutes is typically swapped by a linesman for a freshly frozen one.
NHL officials (via Kukla's Korner): https://kuklaskorner.com/kuklas-korner-hockey/all-about-the-nhl-puck

🟣 **The mood puck:** for 2019–20 the NHL used pucks with a coating that is purple when frozen and turns clear as they warm up.
Cronkite News: https://cronkitenews.azpbs.org/2019/10/07/nhl-hockey-puck-color-change/

💥 **108.8 mph:** Zdeno Chara's slap shot at the 2012 NHL All-Star Skills Competition is still the NHL's hardest-shot record.
Fox Sports / AP: https://www.foxnews.com/sports/chara-breaks-his-record-again-in-hardest-shot
