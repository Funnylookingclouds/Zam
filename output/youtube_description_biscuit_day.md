## Description
A goalie's worst day: 0.2 seconds to react, no masks back in the day, 80 saves and still a loss – and the NHL's biggest blowout ever, 15–0. Biscuit's worst day in under a minute! 🥅😩

⚡ **A fifth of a second:** a 100 mph (≈160 km/h) shot from 30 feet (≈9 m) reaches the net in about 0.2 seconds (30 ft ÷ 146.7 ft/s).

🎭 **The mask:** after a shot from Andy Bathgate hit him in the face on Nov 1, 1959, Montreal's Jacques Plante came back wearing a mask – and became the first NHL goalie to wear one full-time.
NHL.com: https://nhl.com/news/jacques-plante-becomes-first-goalie-to-wear-mask-290431748
CBC: https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.4886259

🧤 **80 saves, still lost:** on March 4, 1941, Chicago's Sam LoPresti made 80 saves on 83 shots – an NHL record – and lost 3–2 to Boston.
NHL.com: https://nhl.com/news/sam-lopresti-faced-83-shots-in-1941-game-for-chicago-339840532
The Hockey News: https://thehockeynews.com/news/news/even-with-80-stops-in-one-nhl-game-sam-lopresti-best-save-came-at-sea

😱 **The worst day ever:** on January 23, 1944, Detroit beat the New York Rangers 15–0 with Ken McAuley in goal – still the most lopsided game in NHL history.
NHL.com: https://www.nhl.com/news/most-lopsided-shutout-nhl-history-detroit-red-wings-fischler-295679094
