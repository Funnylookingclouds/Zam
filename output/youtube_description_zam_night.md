## Description
NHL ice is only about an inch thick, it's painted white, the lines are under the ice, and it's flooded with hot water – Zam's night shift on the ice in under a minute! 🧊🌙

🧊 **About an inch:** NHL ice is typically only around 1 inch (≈2.5 cm) thick – roughly the same as a puck.
AHL: https://theahl.com/news/behind-the-scenes-with-the-ice-makers
HowStuffWorks: https://entertainment.howstuffworks.com/ice-rink4.htm

🎨 **Painted white:** the ice isn't naturally white – a white paint layer goes down early, and the lines and logos are painted between the thin layers of ice. Building a new sheet can take almost two days.
PBS NC: https://www.pbsnc.org/blogs/science/how-a-hockey-rink-is-made/

♨️ **Hot water flood:** rinks have traditionally been flooded with hot water (around 60–71 °C / 140–160 °F); it melts into the surface, bonds and freezes smoother with fewer air bubbles.
ORFA Technical Corner: https://orfa.wildapricot.org/page-1864350
University of Regina: https://edusites.uregina.ca/robhuber/?p=138

🚜 **The first ice resurfacer** was built in 1949 in Paramount, California.
Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_resurfacer
